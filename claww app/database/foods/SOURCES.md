# Food data sources and licences

Every row names its source in `source` and the exact record in `source_ref`. If a source's licence is unclear, do
not copy its values: leave the row out and list it in `docs/food-review.md`.

| Source | Licence | What it supplies | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |  --- |
| USDA FoodData Central, SR Legacy (`FoodData_Central_sr_legacy_food_csv_2018-04.zip` from https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets) | Public domain, CC0 1.0 | Per-100 g nutrients for 112 ingredients (energy, protein, fat, carbohydrate, fibre, sugar, calcium, iron, vitamin C, B12, sodium) | **Imported 2026-11-04** by `tools/import_usda.py`. Each row's `source_ref` has the FDC id and the exact USDA description. Rows marked `PROXY` use the nearest USDA food (for example poha uses raw rice, soya chunks use soy protein concentrate) and need the nutritionist's own value. |
| Authored household recipes | Written for CLAWW | The grams of each ingredient in one standard serving of 86 Indian dishes | **Estimates by the developer, not measurements.** They are the part that most needs the nutritionist (`RECIPES` in `tools/import_usda.py`). Dish nutrition is computed from them, never typed. |
| Indian Food Composition Tables (ICMR-NIN, IFCT 2017) | **Not confirmed.** The tables are a government publication with a copyright notice; reuse terms were not found | Would give native Indian values (paneer, jaggery, curry leaves, millets, many greens) | **Not copied.** Ask NIN for written permission, or ask the nutritionist to supply values. |
| Values the nutritionist provides | Theirs to grant, in writing | Anything above, and the gaps below | Waiting |

## Household units

Katori 150 g (150 ml of a thin dish), roti 40 g, idli 40 g, dosa 80 g, tsp oil or ghee 5 g, tsp sugar 4 g, glass 250 g,
cup of tea 150 g, egg 50 g (USDA large), banana 118 g (USDA medium). These are standard sizes, stated assumptions,
and the nutritionist should confirm them against the sizes their clients use.

When raw meat, greens or vegetables outweigh the cooked serving (they lose water in the pan), the serving is defined by
the raw ingredient weight, so a dish's unit grams always match its nutrients exactly.

## Files

- `ingredients.csv`, `dishes.csv`, `dish_items.csv`, `units.csv`: generated by `tools/import_usda.py`; `build_foods.mjs` validates them and writes `database/seed_foods.sql`.
- To regenerate: download and extract the USDA zip, run `python database/foods/tools/import_usda.py <extracted folder>`, then `node database/foods/build_foods.mjs`.
