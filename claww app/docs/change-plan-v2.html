How the app works after these changes

A normal day looks like this. You open CLAWW and Home greets you for the actual time of day. The top card shows recovery, sleep and nutrition. Under it are three small tiles for steps, calories burned and streak, and each one opens its own card with a graph. Water and sleep each take one slim row instead of two large cards.

When you go to Workouts, the day pills show what already happened this week: a check for completed days, a cross for missed ones, a dot for today. Tapping a past day shows what you did or that you missed it. Tapping today shows your prescription. That prescription is now worked out fresh when you start the session, from your last logs and from how recovered each muscle is, so it isn't frozen inside the plan. Before you start, CLAWW asks two quick questions about the muscles you're training today: how sore they are and how strong they feel. During the session each set has an optional effort chip (reps left in the tank). When you finish, the engine decides the next weight, reps and sets for each exercise and shows you why. The workout player itself becomes a full-screen set list where finishing a set is one tap, and the rest timer keeps running on the lock screen.

Reminders actually arrive. They are tied to your real training days and times, and they stay quiet when you have already done the thing: no "Log lunch" after you logged lunch.

In Nutrition, meal suggestions use your goal, dietary restrictions, food preferences and all four remaining macros, and give three options to choose from. Tapping any logged meal shows the photo you took. The Tracker becomes a performance page, color-coded by area, with strength trends, weekly volume per muscle, a muscle recovery map and a consistency heatmap.

Workouts

Changes 05–08. Today is Thursday in these mockups. Tuesday was a plan day.

05Past days show Completed or Missed, not Begin FIX

Now: the database already records each day as completed or skipped (workout_day_events), but Workouts only uses those records to pick the suggested day. The pills and the Begin button ignore them.

Each day pill gets a state: completed (green check), skipped or missed (red cross), today (blue dot), upcoming (plain) and rest (dimmed). The button under a day depends on that state.

  • Completed: session summary with sets as performed, and the adaptive changes it caused. Button: View full session.
  • Skipped (you tapped Skip) or Missed (the date passed with nothing logged): what the day was, how recovered those muscles are now, and two choices. Do it today instead, or keep today's plan.
  • Today: the computed prescription and Begin Workout.
  • Upcoming: a preview, labelled as a preview. You can still start it early from a menu.

Completed and skipped work with today's data. Missed needs plan days tied to real weekdays, because "Day 1, Day 2" plans have no dates to miss. See decision D13.

06How-to video for every exercise NEW

Version 1, built now: each of the 183 catalog exercises gets 3 numbered form steps, one common mistake, and its primary and secondary muscles, written by us and reviewed, never generated. This costs almost nothing and reuses the catalog fields the adaptive system needs anyway. Version 2, later: a short looping demo clip (8–15 s, front and side angle) in the space at the top of the sheet, stored in Supabase Storage and cached on the phone.

Photos, video or 3D

Video is the right second step, after the text guidance ships. A licensed exercise-video pack or commissioned clips can cover all 183 exercises at a known cost, and it shows real movement, which photos can't. Interactive 3D models cost several times more per exercise and add a heavy 3D renderer to the app. A good later upgrade is a 3D muscle map for the Muscles tab, which also serves the recovery map in the Tracker. Which media source to use is decision D14.

07Manually added exercises get computed sets and reps FIX

Now: to your question, no. Adding an exercise from the picker writes a fixed 3 sets × 10 reps into the plan, with no AI and no adaptation.

An added exercise gets the same numbers the plan's own exercises get. Its rep range comes from your goal, experience and age, and sets come from your recovery. A compound lift added on a strength plan gets something like 3 × 6–8. An isolation lift gets 3 × 10–12. The model still never computes numbers, so this needs no AI call and is instant.

From its first session the exercise is part of the adaptive system like every other one. The first session counts as a calibration session: you pick a weight you can do for the target reps with about 2 left in the tank, and the engine takes over from there. The picker also shows a hint when the exercise overlaps a muscle that is already low on recovery today.

08Calendar stays as it is KEEP

No change to the workout calendar. It already reads the same completed and skipped records, so its colors will now agree with the day pills.

Workout player and reminders

Changes 16 and 17, added after review.

16Workout player rebuilt for speed REDESIGN

Now: the player is a small bar at the bottom of the screen. Each set takes two taps (Finish Set, then Confirm Set), and changing reps or weight means tapping 24 px arrows one rep or 2.5 kg at a time. The rest timer re-renders the whole player every second.

The player opens full screen and is built around one rule: a set that went as planned takes one tap.

  • Set rows. Every set of the current exercise is a row, prefilled with today's prescription and showing last time's numbers. Done logs the set with those numbers instantly; saving happens in the background and never blocks the screen.
  • Fast edits. Tap a number to get a quick picker (common values one tap away, then a wheel), instead of stepping one at a time. Changing set 1's weight carries forward to the remaining sets.
  • Effort chip. The optional reps-left chip sits on the same screen, so it adds no extra step.
  • Rest. A big readable ring with −15 s, +15 s and Skip, next set's numbers, and a lock-screen timer that buzzes when rest ends.
  • Moving around. Swipe left to skip an exercise, swipe right to go back, a list button to jump to any exercise, and Up next always visible. Minimize to a slim bar to check something else in the app.
  • Smoothness. The timer runs in its own small component so the rest of the screen doesn't redraw every second, with haptics on Done and when rest ends. Target: under 100 ms from tap to the next set on a mid-range Android phone.
  • Never lose a session. Sets are saved on the phone first and synced when the network is back, which also covers gyms with bad signal.

17Reminders that actually arrive FIX

Now: there are three fixed local reminders (workout 18:00, lunch 13:00, sleep 22:00). They are only scheduled after you accept a prompt that appears once after your first finished workout, or after you flip the switch in Settings. They fire every day whether or not you trained or logged. No Android notification channel is created, there is no server push at all, and nothing reschedules them after an app update or a phone restart.

Fixing this has two parts.

  • Make local reminders reliable: create a proper Android channel, ask for permission at a sensible moment (end of onboarding, with a clear reason), reschedule every time the app opens, and test on a real Android phone (Expo Go can't test this properly).
  • Make them smart: the workout reminder fires before your actual training time on your actual training days and names today's session. Meal and sleep reminders are cancelled for the day once you log. Each type gets its own time and switch in Settings.
  • Add real push for things the phone can't know: phone push tokens are saved to Supabase, and a scheduled Edge Function sends messages like a missed-day nudge the next morning or a streak about to break. These use Expo's push service, so no separate Firebase code is needed beyond the Android setup.
  • No more than 2 notifications a day in total. Sending more is the quickest way to get them switched off.

09Setup preferences reach meal suggestions FIX

What the code shows: workout generation does read setup: training days, split, workout types, injuries and activity level all go into generate-plan. The gap is on the nutrition side. "Suggest breakfast" only sends your dietary restrictions plus remaining calories and protein. It doesn't know your goal or remaining carbs and fats. It doesn't know what you've already eaten today. Setup never asks about cuisine or foods you dislike, so those can't be used at all.

The suggestion request will carry a full food profile: goal (fat loss, muscle gain, maintain), dietary restrictions, all four remaining macros, meals already logged today (so it doesn't repeat lunch at dinner), and two new optional setup answers: preferred cuisines and foods to avoid. Both can be edited later in Profile, and existing users are asked once, on their next visit to Nutrition. Restrictions stay hard rules. Every suggestion is checked against them after generation, as today.

Your note mentioned "generating the workout" while talking about nutrition. If you also saw a workout plan ignore a setup answer, tell me which one (for example, it trained on a day you didn't pick), because generate-plan does receive those fields. See D15.

13 · The new adaptive system

Built from the two co-founder PDFs: "Adaptive System Thresholds and Commands" and "Muscle Recovery Score". The plan still decides the split and the exercises. The engine decides each exercise's weight, reps and sets every session, from what actually happened.

The big change: numbers are worked out per session

Today, sets and reps are fixed when the plan is generated and only change if you regenerate. In the new system the plan stores each exercise's rep range and starting point. When you tap Begin, CLAWW computes today's prescription for every exercise from your logs and recovery. When you finish, it saves the decision and the reason. All of this is plain math in a shared module; the model never computes a number.

The decision flow

1PerformanceHow did the last session go? Reps achieved against the target, every set.
2RecoveryIs the body ready? Whole-body (sleep, protein) and per-muscle score.
3OverloadWhich single thing goes up next: reps, weight or sets. Recovery can block it.
4ExerciseKeep it, replace it, or suggest a harder variation.
→PrescriptionToday's weight × reps × sets, with a one-line reason.

1 · Performance

Each exercise has a rep range (for example 6–10) and a current rep target inside it. Performance is total reps achieved divided by target × sets.

Weight steps: +2.5 kg for barbell and machine lifts, +1 to 2 kg for dumbbell and isolation lifts. Sets: +1 after repeated success with good recovery, −1 after repeated decline. Bodyweight exercises climb reps to the top of the range, then the engine suggests a harder variation.

Whole-body (PDF 1) uses sleep, protein as a percentage of target, and fatigue.

Per muscle (PDF 2) gives each major muscle its own Estimated Muscle Recovery score out of 100:

Recovery = Fatigue × 0.40 + Time × 0.25 + Soreness × 0.20 + Readiness × 0.15
PartWhere it comes fromScale
Fatigue · 40%Automatic, from your last sessions: effective sets for that muscle, how close to failure (RIR), and the exercise's stimulus type0–100, 100 = fully fresh
Time · 25%Automatic, hours since that muscle was last trained<24h 20 · 24–48 50 · 48–72 75 · 72–96 90 · 96+ 100

The PDF's own example: Fatigue 55, Time 75, Soreness 75, Readiness 85 gives 65.25, so Chest 65 · Moderate. Bands: 85–100 Excellent, 70–84 Good, 50–69 Moderate, 30–49 Low, 0–29 Very low. Each score carries a confidence level, which drops when inputs are missing (no RIR logged, check-in skipped) instead of the engine inventing values. Per the PDF, recovery can change sets, reps and intensity but never changes the split you chose. The same score is used on Home, in the Tracker, in insights and in the engine.

3 · Progressive overload

Only one thing goes up at a time, in this order: reps climb to the top of the range, then weight goes up and reps rebuild from the bottom, and only after repeated cycles does volume (sets) go up. Poor whole-body recovery, or a Low or Very low muscle score, blocks progression for that day: you keep last session's numbers, and the reason says why.

4 · Exercise intelligence

Each exercise is kept, replaced or given a suggested variation. A replacement is triggered by missing equipment, "I can't do this", discomfort, dislike, or a plateau (no progress for several sessions). A replacement must keep the same primary muscle, movement pattern, role (main lift or accessory) and fit your equipment. This needs extra fields in the exercise catalog, described under "What changes underneath" below.

What changes underneath

  • Exercise catalog: each of the 183 exercises gets a primary muscle, secondary muscles, movement pattern, role (main or accessory), whether it's loadable, a stimulus type, possible harder and easier variations, form steps and a media link. I draft these; your co-founder reviews them.
  • Set logs: add set number, RIR and session id to workout_logs. Prescribed and achieved reps and weight are already there.
  • New table for current state: one row per user per exercise, holding the rep target, working weight, sets, and the success and failure counters.
  • New table for check-ins: soreness and readiness per muscle per session.
  • New table for decisions: every prescription with its reason, which feeds the Workouts summary and the Tracker counts.
  • One shared engine module, used by the app and the Edge Functions, with unit tests for every threshold in the PDFs.
  • The old whole-body recovery formula stops using water and "a meal was logged". Water stays a habit on Home but no longer moves recovery.

Old system and new system, in short

Today CLAWW adapts only when a plan is generated or regenerated. It looks at one recovery score made from sleep, rest time, a rough fatigue estimate, water and whether a meal was logged, and moves the maximum sets by one. It looks at the last ten sets and moves reps by two if you were well under or over. The regenerate reasons (too hard, too easy, wrong focus) shift reps by three or change the split. Between regenerations the numbers never move, weight is never prescribed, and the plan doesn't know which muscle is tired.

The new system adapts every session, per exercise. It tracks a rep target and working weight for each exercise and moves exactly one of reps, weight or sets based on what you actually lifted. It also gives each muscle its own recovery score from training load, time, soreness and readiness. Whole-body sleep and protein can hold or reduce the session, a single tired muscle only affects its own exercises, and every change comes with a reason you can read. Exercises can also be swapped for a like-for-like replacement when needed. The plan and split stay yours throughout.

Market fit

The Indian fitness app market was about USD 521 million in 2025, with growth forecast near 19% a year to 2034. Indian users are price-sensitive: the same report suggests ₹99–199 a month in smaller cities and ₹499–999 in big ones. The big local players each own a different lane: HealthifyMe nutrition, cult.fit classes and gyms, Fittr human coaching. Global apps already do adaptive lifting: Fitbod models per-muscle recovery from $12.99 a month, and Alpha Progression and Dr. Muscle adjust per set using reps left in the tank. So the engine alone isn't new. The open space is the combination: adaptive lifting plus Indian food, at Indian prices, for students and young people who lift at a gym.

Three gaps follow from that and are added as the last build phase: Telugu and Hindi in the app, an affordable price tier, and checking photo estimates against real Indian meals before marketing them.
